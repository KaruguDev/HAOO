<objective>
Prove QUAL-01 on the live sites: at every supported width, the Products and HAOO journeys have no
horizontal overflow and no hidden primary actions.

The naive form of this check is the single most dangerous assertion in the phase. Both top-level
wrappers ship a horizontal-clipping utility, so a document-width comparison passes even when content
genuinely overflows — it is a non-assertion wearing the costume of one. The three-part measurement
here is what makes the green mark mean something.

Purpose: turn "the site looks fine on a phone" into a per-element measurement at six widths.
Output: `e2e/viewport.e2e.ts` and `05-EVIDENCE-VIEWPORT.md`.

**Working directory: the HAOO checkout** (05-01, D-01/D-04). The Products region is reached by its
live URL only; nothing is installed in `../ZERO-PAPERHUB`.
</objective>

## Planner assumptions carried forward (surfaced, not dropped)

- **QUAL-01 / unclassified probe edge (unresolved).** The edge probe could not classify QUAL-01 and
  flagged it for manual review. Reviewed here: QUAL-01's shape is a *layout* claim over a closed set
  of widths and a closed set of actions, which is why neither a boundary nor an ordering taxonomy fits
  it. The planner's assumption is that the closed D-09 matrix (six widths) and the closed P1-P13
  action list are together a sufficient sample of "supported mobile and desktop widths" — no
  continuous sweep between widths is performed, so a defect that appears only at, say, 412 px is not
  covered by this evidence. That is a deliberate sampling choice inherited from the owner's matrix,
  not an oversight.

- **UI consideration, zero-one-many / Products collection (unresolved).** The Products section renders
  a featured single-card layout at one product, a two-column grid at two or more, and nothing at zero.
  Only the single-card branch is live, so only that branch is provable on the live Products region.
  The other two branches remain covered by jsdom only. Carried as an explicit assumption: this phase's
  live evidence says nothing about the grid or empty branches.

- **UI consideration, ZERO-PAPER HUB home contact form (unresolved).** That form sits outside the
  Products region, has its own submit, status region and honeypot, and carries a focus indicator
  measured at roughly 1.74:1. It is out of scope by decision D-OQ-3 and is deferred unfixed. Carried
  as an explicit assumption: the ZERO-PAPER HUB contact form is unproven by this phase and does not
  affect the Products journey.

    Extend the spec with the primary-action and mobile-navigation halves.

    **Primary actions.** For each width, for each entry in the closed list whose surface matches the
    surface under test: locate the action by its shipped accessible name, then assert the five
    conditions — it exists in the accessibility tree with a non-empty accessible name; it is visible
    (non-zero bounding box, visibility not hidden, opacity not zero, not clipped entirely out of its
    layout box); its bounding box lies within the viewport's horizontal extent; its hit target is at
    least the primary floor of 44 by 44 CSS pixels; and it is not disabled. Call the list's vacuity
    guard first, so a selector that matched nothing fails loudly instead of passing over an empty set.

    Scrolling to an action before measuring it is expected and correct. Do **not** write an
    above-the-fold assertion: the owner decided that no hidden primary actions means reachable, not
    visible without scrolling, because requiring above-the-fold at the narrowest width would force a
    redesign of shipped screens that this phase's boundary forbids.

    For an action behind a collapsed disclosure — the mobile navigation contents and the measurement
    disclosure's body — assert the opening control first: it is keyboard-operable, has a non-empty
    accessible name, and meets the same 44-pixel floor. Only then open it and assert the revealed
    action. An action behind a control that itself fails the floor is not reachable.

    Where the same accessible name renders more than once, assert the destinations are identical
    rather than asserting the names are unique — several actions render three times with
    byte-identical names by design, and a uniqueness assertion would fail a correct page.

    Assert the 24-by-24 floor for interactive elements that are not in the primary list, recording
    both numbers separately in the evidence so a reader does not conflate the stricter shipped bar
    with the standard's minimum.

    **Mobile navigation.** At the two mobile widths: the desktop navigation is not in the layout, the
    toggle is present and named, its expanded-state attribute reads collapsed initially, its controls
    attribute resolves to an element that exists, and that element's hidden state tracks the toggle.
